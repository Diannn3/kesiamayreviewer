Ten principles to carry into every question

  1. KYC is not just identity collection; it also covers ownership, purpose, risk, screening, ongoing monitoring and refresh.
  2. Obtaining information and verifying information are different steps.
  3. A customer statement is evidence, but it is not always sufficient corroboration.
  4. Beneficial ownership looks through legal entities to natural persons and can arise through control as well as ownership.
  5. A screening alert is not a confirmed match; validate identifiers and document the result.
  6. A PEP is not automatically suspicious; PEP controls are preventive and risk-based.
  7. Adverse media is assessed for reliability, relevance, recency and corroboration; it is not automatically proof.
  8. A red flag is a reason to investigate, not a conviction.
  9. High risk is not automatically unacceptable risk; apply EDD, governance and risk appetite.
  10. KYC is dynamic: material new information or behavior can trigger review before the next periodic date.

01

Domain 1

Identify and Verify the Customer

Build a reliable picture of who the customer is, who owns or controls them, why the relationship exists, and whether later activity still fits that picture.

Competency map

CompetencyWhat you should be able to do
1.1Determine what information is needed for the customer type and use appropriate research sources to close gaps.
1.2Complete appropriate customer verification procedures.
1.3Assess the nature and purpose of the account or relationship, including shell-company and tax-compliance concerns.
1.4Identify ultimate beneficial owners (UBOs) and relevant connected or third parties.
1.5Reconsider prior KYC information when account activity or circumstances change.

Core knowledge

Information sufficiency

Collect information proportionate to the customer type and risk. A retail individual, operating company, charity, trust, fund and holding company require different evidence.

Verification

Do not confuse obtaining information with verifying it. Verification uses reliable, independent evidence or a controlled verification process to establish that the information is credible.

Purpose and intended nature

Understand what the relationship is for, what activity is expected, who the counterparties are likely to be, and why the selected products/channels make sense.

Beneficial ownership

Trace legal ownership through layers to natural persons and consider control through other means when ownership percentages do not tell the whole story.

Dynamic KYC

The onboarding story is a hypothesis that must remain consistent with later activity. Material changes should trigger reassessment rather than waiting mechanically for the next scheduled review.

UBO decision tree

  1. Map direct legal owners.
  2. Trace each corporate owner through intermediate layers.
  3. Identify natural persons with ultimate ownership interests under the applicable framework.
  4. If ownership does not reveal the controller, examine control through other means: voting rights, appointment rights, contractual control, and similar mechanisms.
  5. For trusts or arrangements, identify the relevant settlor, trustee, protector, beneficiaries or classes, and persons exercising ultimate effective control, as applicable.
  6. Resolve conflicting records before closing the KYC case.

High-yield notes

  • Customer-provided information starts the profile; independent evidence tests or corroborates material facts.
  • The exact evidence needed depends on customer type, risk, product and channel. Avoid universal checklists that ignore context.
  • For legal persons: understand legal existence, business activity, ownership/control and authorized persons.
  • For legal arrangements: identify relevant parties and any person exercising ultimate effective control.
  • For non-face-to-face onboarding: consider impersonation, document authenticity and whether channel-specific controls are adequate.

Worked scenario

A company is 60% owned by HoldCo. HoldCo is owned 55% by Ana and 45% by many small shareholders. The direct shareholder is HoldCo, but the analyst should trace through HoldCo and consider Ana as an ultimate natural-person owner. If a shareholder agreement gives another person dominant control, that control must also be analyzed.

02

Domain 2

Screen the Customer

Use sanctions, PEP and adverse-information sources intelligently. A screening alert is a lead to validate, not a conclusion.

Competency map

CompetencyWhat you should be able to do
2.1Screen the customer and relevant parties for sanctions risk.
2.2Use primary and secondary sources, including adverse media, while assessing reliability and relevance.
2.3Distinguish material from immaterial screening/monitoring hits and validate potential matches appropriately.

Core knowledge

Sanctions screening

Screen the appropriate persons/entities and use current lists and institution policy. Existing relationships may also require rescreening as lists or customer data change.

Source quality

Primary official records generally carry more evidentiary weight than anonymous or unsupported allegations. Relevance, recency, independence and corroboration matter.

Hit validation

Compare identifiers such as full name, aliases, date of birth, nationality, address, registration data and other unique identifiers before deciding that a hit is a match or false positive.

Screening validation workflow

  1. Identify which list or source produced the alert.
  2. Compare names and aliases.
  3. Compare strong identifiers: DOB, nationality, address, registration/passport/tax ID, place of birth or other unique data where available.
  4. Assess whether the differences are explainable or exclude the match.
  5. Document the evidence and decision.
  6. Escalate potentially true or material matches under the applicable procedure.

High-yield notes

  • Stronger evidence: competent-authority action, court record, official filing, reliable primary document.
  • Useful but contextual: reputable investigative reporting that identifies evidence and subjects clearly.
  • Weaker evidence: anonymous post, unsourced allegation, repost or aggregator with no original evidence.
  • Never use source quality alone: identity match, relevance, seriousness, recency and corroboration still matter.

Worked scenario

A customer shares a name with a sanctioned person, but DOB, nationality and address all differ. Do not clear the case merely because the name is common, and do not confirm the match merely because the name is identical. Validate the full identifier set, document the analysis, and follow policy.

03

Domain 3

Rate the Customer's Risk

Translate facts into a defensible risk view across customer, product, geography, channel and behavior, then compare the result with risk appetite and escalation rules.

Competency map

CompetencyWhat you should be able to do
3.1Evaluate customer, product, country/geographic and channel risk.
3.2Recognize and explain red flags for money laundering, sanctions evasion, bribery/corruption, tax evasion and related financial crime.
3.3Analyze risk associated with assets under management (AUM) and whether the scale of assets is coherent with the customer profile.
3.4Assess the risk score against enterprise risk assessment, risk appetite and escalation requirements.

Core knowledge

Risk dimensions

Avoid single-factor risk decisions. Consider the combination of customer, product/service, geography, delivery channel, ownership, expected activity and other institution-specific factors.

Red flags

A red flag is an indicator that warrants inquiry; it is not automatically proof of financial crime. Look for patterns, inconsistency, concealment and lack of economic rationale.

AUM

The scale and provenance of assets should make sense against the customer's occupation, business, financial history and declared source of wealth.

Risk appetite and overrides

Models support decisions but do not replace judgment. Material risks may require manual escalation or override, and overrides themselves require governance and documentation.

Risk-rating questions

  1. Customer — What entity or person is this? Occupation/business? Ownership complexity? PEP/adverse information?
  2. Product/service — What can the product do? High value? Cross-border? Cash intensive? Private banking? Crypto or complex investment access?
  3. Geography — Where is the customer based or operating? Where are counterparties and funds moving?
  4. Channel — Face-to-face, remote, intermediated, digital, agent-based? How strong are identity and fraud controls?
  5. Activity — Do expected volumes, counterparties and transaction types make sense?
  6. Controls — What mitigants exist, are they effective, and do they reduce residual risk enough to fit risk appetite?

High-yield notes

  • A good CKYCA answer avoids two extremes: ignoring suspicious facts and declaring guilt from a single indicator.
  • Identify the pattern, ask what evidence would explain or confirm it, and escalate according to materiality and policy.

Worked scenario

A customer repeatedly sends round-dollar transfers to unrelated counterparties in countries outside its stated market. That pattern is a red flag. The correct KYC move is to understand the economic rationale and counterparties and compare the activity with the customer profile — not to call the customer a money launderer solely from the pattern.

Domain 4

Perform Enhanced Due Diligence

When risk is higher or information is more complex, obtain stronger evidence, understand wealth and ownership, review actual activity, and escalate decisions appropriately.

Competency map

CompetencyWhat you should be able to do
4.1Identify and obtain information needed to establish and corroborate source of wealth.
4.2Examine complex ownership structures to identify the natural persons who ultimately own or control the customer.
4.3Use the available evidence to recommend retaining, exiting or escalating a relationship for further EDD, consistent with policy and applicable law.
4.4Perform account-activity reviews and assess whether actual activity is consistent with the stated profile.

Core knowledge

Source of wealth

Explain how the customer accumulated their overall wealth. Corroboration should be proportionate to risk and may include business-sale evidence, audited financials, inheritance documents, tax records, property records or investment statements.

Complex ownership

Layering, nominees, trusts and cross-border entities require the analyst to trace through intermediate entities rather than stopping at the first corporate shareholder.

Retain, escalate or exit

High risk does not always mean exit, and adverse media does not automatically equal guilt. Base recommendations on evidence, legal obligations, control effectiveness and the institution's risk appetite.

Activity review

Compare observed behavior with the stated purpose, expected volumes, counterparties and geographies. Investigate material deviations and update the profile when facts have changed.

EDD decision discipline

  1. Identify exactly why risk is elevated.
  2. List unresolved facts or evidence gaps.
  3. Obtain additional evidence proportionate to the risk.
  4. Reassess ownership/control and SoW/SoF where relevant.
  5. Review account/activity behavior for consistency.
  6. Determine whether risks are understood and within risk appetite.
  7. Escalate for senior or specialized approval where required.
  8. Document retain/exit/restrict recommendations and the basis.

High-yield notes

  • Source of Wealth (SoW) explains how the customer accumulated overall wealth or net worth.
  • Source of Funds (SoF) explains where the specific money or assets used in a relationship or transaction came from.
  • Examples of SoW: business ownership/sale, career earnings, inheritance, investment gains, property portfolio.
  • Examples of SoF: salary payment, sale proceeds, bank transfer, dividend, trust distribution, loan proceeds.

Worked scenario

A wealthy customer has transparent ownership and credible SoW but one serious article from a reputable source alleges corruption. A defensible response is to validate the allegation, assess relevance and identity, perform EDD and escalate as appropriate. Automatic exit and automatic dismissal are both weaker than evidence-based review.

05

Domain 5

Create and Maintain the Customer Profile

Convert evidence and decisions into a precise, current and auditable profile that another analyst can understand and rely on.

Competency map

CompetencyWhat you should be able to do
5.1Apply data-privacy requirements and understand how inaccurate or stale data can distort KYC decisions.
5.2Create customer profiles that are objective, precise, evidence-based and useful to downstream reviewers.
5.3Create a defensible audit trail showing sources, decisions, approvals and changes.
5.4Review and update the customer profile through periodic reviews and event-driven triggers.

Core knowledge

Privacy and data quality

Use customer data only in authorized systems and for permitted purposes. Incorrect data can create false positives, missed matches and incorrect risk ratings.

Objective writing

Separate facts, customer statements, external evidence, analyst assessment and unresolved gaps. Avoid loaded or conclusory language that is not supported by evidence.

Audit trail

Record what was reviewed, when, by whom, which sources were used, how exceptions were resolved and who approved material decisions.

Refresh

Periodic reviews are the backstop; event-driven reviews respond to material changes such as ownership changes, new adverse information, unusual activity or a significant change in business.

Objective profile-writing formula

  1. CUSTOMER STATEMENT
  2. VERIFIED FACT
  3. DISCREPANCY / GAP
  4. ANALYST ASSESSMENT
  5. ACTION / DECISION
  6. SOURCE / DATE / APPROVAL

High-yield notes

  • Change in ownership/control or directors.
  • Material change in business, products, geographies or expected activity.
  • New sanctions/PEP/adverse-information result.
  • Unusual transaction behavior inconsistent with the profile.
  • Major change in source of funds/wealth or financial condition relevant to the relationship.
  • Material data-quality error or contradiction discovered.

Worked scenario

A customer changes majority ownership and expands into higher-risk countries two months after a periodic review. Waiting until the next scheduled review would preserve stale KYC. The ownership and geographic change should trigger a targeted refresh now.

Cross-domain review

Integrated scenario logic

PEPs: preventive controls, not a guilt label

PEP status indicates exposure to heightened corruption risk because of prominent public functions. It does not mean the person committed wrongdoing. Risk-based measures can include establishing whether the customer/UBO is a PEP, obtaining appropriate approvals where required, understanding source of wealth/funds and applying enhanced ongoing monitoring in higher-risk cases. Exact legal requirements vary by jurisdiction.

Beneficial ownership: ownership + control

Do not stop at a company that appears on the shareholder register. Trace through intermediate entities to natural persons. Where ownership alone does not identify the controller, examine voting rights, appointment/removal powers, agreements or other means of control.

Shell companies

A shell entity can have legitimate uses, but limited physical operations, opacity, nominee arrangements and unexplained cross-border flows can increase risk. Understand legitimate purpose, ownership/control and economic rationale rather than assuming shell automatically means criminal.

Escalation vs rejection

Immediate rejection can be too aggressive while passive approval can be too weak. Escalation is often appropriate when material facts remain unresolved, risk exceeds an analyst's authority, a policy exception may be required, or specialized sanctions/EDD review is needed.

Assess → Explore → Organize → Present

Use the official-study-guide workflow as a reasoning discipline. Assess what is known and missing; Explore targeted reliable sources; Organize relevant facts, inconsistencies and risk; Present a precise, defensible profile and recommendation. Difficult questions often punish jumping to research or a final decision before defining the actual gap.

Four-eyes, second line, and specialist escalation

A second reviewer can strengthen a first-line decision, but a four-eyes check is not automatically the same as independent second-line oversight. When risk, policy exceptions, sanctions, suspicious activity or specialist expertise exceed the analyst's authority, route the case to the function with the proper independence and mandate.

Intermediaries and gatekeepers

Lawyers, accountants, money managers and other intermediaries can provide useful information, but their involvement does not automatically transfer the institution's KYC responsibility. Understand the underlying customer, assess the intermediary's role and due-diligence quality where relevant, and resolve material ownership, purpose and source-of-wealth gaps.

Trade, tax and cross-border structures

Complex trade flows, tax-efficient structures and cross-border ownership are not inherently illicit. The harder analytical task is to identify mismatches: unexplained third-party payments, inconsistent documents, commercial purpose that does not fit the structure, unusual routing, or facts suggesting concealment rather than legitimate planning.

Research enough — not research forever

More research is not automatically better. Define the decision-relevant question, use the strongest proportionate sources, reconcile material contradictions, and stop when the evidence is sufficient for the required decision or escalation. Broad unfocused searching can waste time and still miss the core KYC gap.

Scenario-answering method

  1. Identify the customer type and exact control being tested.
  2. Separate facts from assumptions and allegations.
  3. Find the material gap or inconsistency.
  4. Ask whether the question wants the FIRST action, BEST action, evidence, escalation or final decision.
  5. Prefer proportionate, evidence-based action over automatic rejection or automatic acceptance.
  6. Use policy/governance for overrides, sanctions and high-risk decisions.
  7. Preserve auditability: corrections, clearances and exceptions should be documented.

Which review conclusion is most defensible?

49A global bank centralizes KYC review in another country. 50An internal quality review finds that a customer's date of birth was entered incorrectly three years ago. The wrong date was used in repeated sanctions and PEP screening and in risk scoring. The correct date is supported by reliable documents. Which remediation is most defensible?
51A KYC investigator is reviewing a complex customer and wants to copy documents to a local desktop folder and a personal encrypted USB drive so they can work while traveling. 52An analyst must summarize a high-risk customer's adverse-media issue. Which profile wording is strongest?
